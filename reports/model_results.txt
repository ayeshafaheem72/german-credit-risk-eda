Honest read:
  - The majority-class baseline already scores 70% accuracy by predicting 'good' for everyone -- any model must be judged against that floor, not against 0%.
  - The unpruned Decision Tree overfits clearly (train accuracy near-perfect, large train/test gap); it is not a usable model as-is.
  - class_weight='balanced' consistently trades accuracy for recall on the bad-risk class -- exactly the trade a lender using the published cost matrix (missed defaulter costs 5x a wrongly-declined good customer) should want, but it is a real trade, not a free lunch: precision on 'bad' drops when recall rises.
  - Business cost (5*FN + 1*FP on 200 test cases) is reported alongside standard metrics because accuracy/F1 alone do not reflect this dataset's stated cost asymmetry -- a model can have a *worse* F1 but a *lower* business cost.
